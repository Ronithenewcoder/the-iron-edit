START HERE

Your blood has a delivery system.

Red blood cells carry oxygen throughout the body using hemoglobin. Anemia occurs when you do not have enough healthy red blood cells or hemoglobin to meet the body's needs.

01

Red blood cells

Think of them as tiny delivery vehicles moving through your bloodstream.

02
Fe

Iron + hemoglobin

Iron is needed to make hemoglobin, the protein that helps red blood cells carry oxygen.

03

When delivery drops

Fatigue, weakness, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, pale skin, and a fast or irregular heartbeat can occur.

Urgent red flags: chest pain, fainting, severe trouble breathing, or significant bleeding deserve urgent medical evaluation.

THE PLOT TWIST

Anemia is a category,
not one diagnosis.

MCV measures average red-cell size. It helps classify the pattern, while history and additional testing help identify the cause.

✦

Quick correction: microcytic cells are not simply “too small to carry iron.” Iron deficiency can reduce normal hemoglobin production and often produces smaller red cells.

IRON DEFICIENCY

Too little available iron for normal hemoglobin production.

B12 / PERNICIOUS

B12 deficiency can cause anemia; pernicious anemia involves impaired B12 absorption.

HEMOLYTIC

Red cells are destroyed faster than the body replaces them.

APLASTIC

Bone marrow does not produce enough new blood cells.

INHERITED

Includes conditions such as sickle cell disease and thalassemia.

CHRONIC / KIDNEY

Chronic disease, inflammation, and kidney problems can contribute to anemia.

WHO'S AT RISK?

Some bodies need a closer look.

Risk does not equal diagnosis. It tells the healthcare team where to look more carefully.

01

Blood loss

Heavy menstrual bleeding, gastrointestinal bleeding, surgery, injury, or frequent blood donation can reduce iron and red blood cells.

02

Higher needs

Pregnancy and periods can increase iron needs or losses.

03

Nutrition + absorption

Low intake of iron, vitamin B12, or folate—or problems absorbing nutrients—can contribute.

04

Health + family history

Older age, kidney disease, chronic inflammation, infection, cancer, autoimmune disease, inherited conditions, and some medicines or treatments can matter.

TREAT THE CAUSE

Same symptom.
Different treatment.

IRON DEFICIENCY→

Oral iron is common; IV iron may be used in selected patients.

B12 DEFICIENCY→

Vitamin B12 replacement; some patients may need injections.

FOLATE DEFICIENCY→

Folate replacement after appropriate evaluation.

OTHER ANEMIAS→

Cause-specific therapy, sometimes with specialist care.

NOT THE SAME THING

IV Iron

Iron is delivered directly through a vein.

VS

NOT THE SAME THING

Blood Transfusion

Donor blood is delivered through an IV line. It may be used in some severe anemias depending on the clinical situation.

!

Don't self-prescribe iron: not every anemia is iron deficiency, and excess iron can be harmful.
